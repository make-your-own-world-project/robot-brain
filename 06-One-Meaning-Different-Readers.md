# Explain the same truth to different readers

![The same supported meaning can be explained in forms suited to different readers.](illustrations/human-protocol-weave.png)

A statement can be accurate and still be useless to the person reading it. The answer may arrive too late, use unfamiliar language, or bury one useful fact under pages of explanation.

This work separates two decisions:

1. What does the evidence support?
2. What is the clearest way to explain it to this reader?

Changing the explanation must not change the evidence.

## Ask instead of guessing

For a particular exchange, the reader may choose a short answer, a detailed explanation, text, audio, or another available form. They can ask for more detail, correct a misunderstanding, or stop.

That choice applies to the exchange. It is not permission to build a permanent personality profile or guess at someone's private mental state.

## Presentation is not truth

Once the supported claims are clear, the writing can change the order, examples, level of detail, and explanation of unfamiliar terms.

It cannot turn a possible interpretation into a fact. It cannot remove a failure that the reader needs in order to understand the result. It cannot add confidence for the sake of sounding helpful.

## Reading time is a real cost

The paid assistants used during development often produced far more text than requested. They repeated points, expanded short statements, and emphasized nearly everything. The person then had to search through the answer, correct it, and explain the request again.

That time matters as much as service fees and computing time. More words are not more helpful when they make the answer harder to find.

A good response leads with the useful result. It gives the explanation needed to understand and use that result. Extra depth remains available when the reader asks for it.

## What still needs testing

The current software can set requirements for length, evidence, and the finished form. Future tests need to compare how well the same supported meaning survives across different readers, languages, cultures, and accessibility needs.

The people receiving an explanation must be able to correct it. Their correction becomes part of the record rather than a hidden preference guessed by a model.
