# Keep private history under the person's control

![A person's full record stays under that person's control while outside help receives only what one task requires.](illustrations/human-knowledge-sovereignty-inversion.png)

A language-model service becomes more useful when it knows more about a person. That creates a strong pull to send the provider an ever larger share of someone's work, relationships, habits, and history.

The provider controls the service. It decides how the model changes, how much earlier information remains available, which rules apply, and whether the product continues to exist.

The fuller history stays on equipment controlled by the person using it. An online model can receive the material needed for one approved task. It does not need the rest of the person's life.

## Why this also protects meaning

Language-model training mixes lessons from many human works. The model may keep useful patterns while losing the full circumstances around each work: who made it, why, for whom, with what evidence, and what happened later.

Calling that loss destructive describes what cannot be recovered from the trained model. It is not an accusation of theft and does not decide whether any particular use was lawful. Copyright, permission, contracts, and other legal questions depend on the facts of each use.

Keeping a person's records outside the model avoids repeating that loss. The original words stay available. Later interpretations remain identifiable as later interpretations.

## Less information leaves the private record

When outside help is useful, the request builder can include only the relevant passages and instructions. The online service does not receive unrelated history or the local methods used to prepare later requests.

This reduces exposure. It does not replace checking the provider's current terms, privacy practices, retention rules, or the law.

## A person's control stops at another person's rights

Personal records often mention other people. Keeping a conversation or observation does not grant permission to publish it, study it for a new purpose, sell it, or use it to speak for someone else.

Another person's name, words, image, location, routine, health, employment, beliefs, and private circumstances may still be protected. The same is true of guesses about that person.

Something being visible online does not create consent. The ability to copy it does not create consent either.

## Keep speakers and claims separate

A record that someone made a statement proves only that the statement was made, when the source supports that event. It does not prove that the statement was true or that the speaker still agrees with it.

Human words, quotations, model-generated text, later notes, corrections, and endorsements are kept as different things. A chat page or assistant does not gain the right to speak for a person merely because it took part in the exchange.

## Public release is separate work

Removing a few names does not automatically make a private record safe to publish. Other details can identify someone when they are combined.

A public version needs its own review for:

- personal and identifying information
- other people's words and rights
- employer and customer information
- passwords, access details, and private locations
- licenses and permission for outside material
- claims that could be mistaken for accusations or professional advice

Approval applies to the exact files reviewed. Automated scans can help find risks, but they cannot grant permission or replace legal advice when it is needed.
