# Keep the correction without erasing the mistake

![Earlier evidence, later understanding, and changes remain connected over time.](illustrations/temporal-meaning-preserved-history.png)

Understanding changes. New evidence can correct an earlier conclusion. A later test can show that the first explanation was wrong. That does not make the earlier record worthless.

The mistake may explain why a decision was made, why time was lost, or why a safeguard now exists. Rewriting the old note would hide that history.

## One event can have several dates

The date on a file is not always the date of the event inside it. A useful record may need to distinguish:

- when something happened
- when someone wrote it down
- when the file was saved here
- when a later interpretation was made
- when a conclusion became current or was replaced

If one of those dates is not known, it stays unknown.

## A simple example

An early note blames a broken part. A later test shows that a power setting was the more likely cause.

The corrected history should keep the early diagnosis, the later test, and the new conclusion. It should show why the conclusion changed. It should not edit the first note to make it look as though everyone knew the answer from the beginning.

## Why the newest model is not the next chapter

Language models change too. A new model was built from a different mix of sources, languages, labels, human feedback, and product rules. It may perform better overall while losing an older cultural reference, local practice, joke, or minority interpretation.

The new answer is not a complete update to the old model's knowledge. It is a new answer from a different model.

Each finding records which model produced it, what the model was shown, and when it answered. A newer answer can be added without silently replacing the earlier one.

## Leave honest gaps

Sometimes the record shows that something changed but not why. In that case, the reason remains open.

A visible gap is more useful than a confident story invented to make the timeline feel complete.
