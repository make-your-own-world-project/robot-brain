# Help without handing over private records

![People can contribute through limited paths without surrendering control of their own work.](illustrations/open-work-collaboration.png)

Someone can help this project without seeing anyone's private history or agreeing with every part of the design.

A useful contribution might repeat one test, challenge one claim, improve a small tool, find a missing viewpoint, or show that a simpler method works better.

## Work that would help

- Repeat a documented test with independently controlled material.
- Test whether one named method performs better than a simple comparison method.
- Improve how sources, corrections, and relationships are preserved.
- Find clearer ways to show where a claim came from and what remains uncertain.
- Measure how much time people spend reading and correcting results.
- Identify assumptions tied to language, culture, disability, profession, or field of study.
- Explore ways for separately controlled installations to exchange only what one task needs.
- Publish a failed test clearly enough that someone else can learn from it.

## What a contribution should explain

A useful report states the question, the material used, permission to use that material, the method, the conditions, the result, and the known weaknesses.

It should also say whether the work is an idea, an isolated test, a test of connected parts, or something running in ordinary use. Those states are not interchangeable.

## What contributors keep

Contributors do not need to transfer private records, use the same language-model provider, accept one worldview, or give this project control over unrelated work.

Their material can remain under their control. A shared result should identify what may be exchanged, why, and under which terms.

## Do not submit

Do not send private records, passwords, access keys, employer or customer information, another person's private information, restricted material, or anything whose rights are unclear.

Any future public submission process will need its own privacy, security, and rights review before it opens.
