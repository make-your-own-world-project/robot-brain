# Words used throughout these documents

![A small set of shared words points back to one lasting record.](illustrations/architecture-glossary.png)

These definitions explain how common words are used in this project.

**Source** — The conversation, note, image, task, document, or other item that arrived. Later work does not overwrite it.

**Working copy** — Text, a description, a list, or another version made for search or review. It can be remade and points back to the source.

**Focused local reader** — A local method with one stated subject, such as language structure, reasoning, time, or possible relationships between passages. It reports findings from saved material rather than acting as a general assistant.

**Finding** — Something a person, model, or focused local reader reported after examining named material. A finding may later be accepted, corrected, disputed, or rejected.

**Work record** — The history of one request: what was asked, what material was used, which working parts took part, what they returned, what failed, and what was kept.

**Source history** — Information about where something came from and how it changed.

**Recorded viewpoint** — What a person or tool could establish at a particular time. Saving it does not make it permanently true.

**General-knowledge reading** — A dated explanation made by a local language model after a conversation has ended. It supplies broad background that helps connect findings from other local methods. It remains a suggestion.

**Request builder** — The part that gathers the sources and findings needed for one answer or document. It may prepare work for a fixed local process, a local model, an online model, or no model.

**Chat screen** — One way to submit work and read results. The included LibreChat fork is a chat screen, not the memory or decision-maker.

**Lost meaning during model training** — A language model keeps patterns learned from human work but cannot rebuild every work with its creator, purpose, evidence, disagreement, and later history attached.

**Context** — The lived and recorded circumstances that explain why something mattered: the people, purpose, time, place, experience, evidence, relationships, and changes around it. Here, context does not simply mean more text placed in a prompt.

**Bias** — A repeated influence on what is seen, favored, omitted, or produced. It can come from society, archives, language coverage, translation, labels, human feedback, business goals, product rules, or missing communities.

**Document requirements** — What the finished work is for, who will read it, what it must cover, what evidence it needs, and how much time it should take to read.

**Rebuilding earlier work** — Gathering the saved source and findings again without asking the original online assistant to remember or repeat them.

**Required check** — A check that must pass before work can be accepted or published.

**Limited outside request** — Material chosen for one job and sent to an outside service without sending the person's complete saved history.

**Control of records** — Practical control over where records are kept, who may use them, what may be revealed, and which interpretations are accepted.

**Replaceable model** — A local or online language model used for a limited contribution. Replacing it does not remove the source, history, earlier findings, or accepted work.

**Unknown** — The evidence does not support a more specific answer. Unknown is not the same as false.
