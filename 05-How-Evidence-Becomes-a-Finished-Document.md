# Build the document before writing the prose

![A document is planned, written, checked, and accepted in separate steps.](illustrations/artifact-assembly-line.png)

Giving a language model a request and a pile of files often produces a document that sounds finished before it is sound. Important evidence may be missing. The same point may appear in several sections. Smooth filler can hide the one sentence the reader needed.

Robot Brain plans the result before asking a language model or another writing method to draft it.

## Start with the reader and the purpose

A maintenance guide, a public explanation, a research summary, and a personal history are different jobs. Before gathering material, the request identifies:

- who will read the document
- what they need from it
- what questions it must answer
- which evidence is required
- how much detail is reasonable

## Find the evidence each section needs

The plan works backward from those questions. It looks for the events, claims, decisions, failed attempts, corrections, and open questions needed to answer them.

Each finding points back to its source. If the available readers cannot support an answer, the plan leaves the question open.

## Make one plan for the whole document

Earlier attempts retrieved passages with similar wording and treated the pile as an explanation. Other attempts asked a language model to write sections separately. The result repeated itself, changed terms between sections, and invented connections that were not in the evidence.

A whole-document plan makes those problems visible before the prose is written. It also decides what can be left out without breaking the story.

## Write only what the evidence can carry

The writing stage orders the material, explains unfamiliar terms, and connects the sections for the intended reader.

A language model may help with the wording. It cannot add a fact, motive, or level of certainty that the sources do not support. A smoother draft is not an improvement if it becomes less accurate or takes longer to read.

## Check the complete document

The final review asks whether the document answers the required questions, uses its sources honestly, keeps important uncertainty visible, and fits the reader's available time.

What gets accepted is the exact finished document that passed those checks. A collection of individually successful paragraphs is not enough.
