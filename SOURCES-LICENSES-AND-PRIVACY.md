# Sources, licenses, and public-release checks

## Influence is different from included material

This project credits work that shaped the design, including approaches that were only studied or compared. Like a musician naming influences, that credit explains part of the project's history. It does not mean the credited work was copied into this release.

The [United States Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/) explains that copyright protects original expression rather than ideas, procedures, processes, systems, methods, concepts, principles, or discoveries. The review therefore separates acknowledgement of an influence from the use or distribution of protected material.

When the project uses, changes, copies, embeds, or distributes a protected component, the terms for that component are checked for that use.

This release contains noncommercial public documentation and project-created illustrations. It does not contain outside source code, trained language-model files, books, papers, research-collection text, posts, recordings, or scans.

## What the credit records

The historical record says whether outside work was adopted, tested, compared, rejected, or only studied. The public credit gives the name, creator or responsible group, a dependable link when available, and a short explanation of the influence.

The acknowledgement lists do not reproduce the named works. Their authors and organizations were not asked to endorse the project, and being listed does not imply participation.

## Material used for the main writing study

The private Weave records identify public-domain works from Project Gutenberg, public-domain historical newspapers, speeches, and government works as the main writing collection. A small group of public standards expressly released for public use was examined separately.

This work measured and compared writing. It did not train a new general language model. The retained records contain observations, public source locations, and short digital fingerprints used to confirm the material examined. They do not keep copies of the source text.

The same records preserve exclusions and failed attempts instead of silently replacing them. They also keep later work separate:

- LibriVox timing records were consulted, but no audio was downloaded or processed.
- A large Project Gutenberg collection released under CC BY 4.0 was audited at the index level; its book archives were not downloaded or measured.
- Later scientific, music, and model tests have their own source and rights records. They are not evidence that the main writing study used those materials for training.
- A later eight-song rights review cleared seven works under its stated multi-country rule and rejected one uncertain work. The rejected work was not admitted into a cleared collection. That later review remains separate from the confirmed public-domain writing study.

Earlier wording placed confirmed study material and broader influences in the same list. This version separates them. Finding something online does not by itself grant permission to reuse it. The description of the writing study comes from the private source records.

## Private-information review

The reviewed English files contain no private file location, username, personal email address, password, access key, or private network address. They also contain no employer name, customer name, family detail, health detail, schedule, private quotation, or instruction for reaching private records or running services.

Public names appear only as credit or to identify a published program, research group, archive, provider, or collection. Mention does not imply employment, participation, approval, partnership, or endorsement.
