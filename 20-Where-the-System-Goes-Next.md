# What comes next

The current software already demonstrates the central idea: a paid language model can help with a task without becoming the permanent home of the person's records.

The next work is not a larger centralized model or a local model trained on someone's life. It is better testing, clearer use, and safer exchange between records that remain under different people's control.

## Independent testing

The strongest next step is for an independent group to test the same design with different records, people, tools, and language-model services.

That work must protect private information and avoid copying private details from the current installation.

## Stronger evidence for relationships

More tests are needed for links between events, corrections, identities over time, and the meaning attached to a source.

Future relationship checks must distinguish a supported connection from one that merely sounds likely. When a date, identity, or cause cannot be established, the maintained record should leave the question open.

The completed-conversation milestone brought every retained contribution for one exchange into a record that can be rebuilt. Future tests will apply the same method to more conversations, conflicting accounts, later corrections, and other kinds of material.

## Clearer ways to inspect the record

People need to see sources, findings, corrections, omissions, cost, and approval without reading internal program records.

Screens should lead with the useful result, show where it came from, and explain problems in ordinary language.

## More languages and cultures

Future work will test records from more languages, cultures, places, and forms of knowledge.

Translation must preserve where an idea came from and identify ideas that do not fit neatly into the project's usual words. Fluent speakers and relevant communities need a way to correct an interpretation without giving up control of their own knowledge.

When a provider does not reveal what shaped a model, the missing information should remain unknown. It should not be treated as culturally neutral or historically complete.

## Exchange without surrender

Installations controlled by different people or groups should be able to exchange approved work without transferring all of their private records.

An exchange needs to preserve the source, purpose, permitted use, uncertainty, corrections, and who supplied the material. Neither side should gain hidden power to decide what the other side's records mean.

## Honest cost records

Tool choice should include service fees, usage limits, energy, waiting, retries, upkeep, and the time people spend finding and correcting mistakes.

A powerful online model may still be the right tool. The record should make clear why the result justified its full cost.

## Public documentation

Future releases will continue to receive separate checks for readable language, sources, rights, privacy, employment information, illustrations, and translations.

Each public claim will identify the evidence behind it. Each release will name the exact files and destination approved for publication.

The goal is not to stop using commercial language models. It is to stop depending on any one of them to preserve a person's history.
